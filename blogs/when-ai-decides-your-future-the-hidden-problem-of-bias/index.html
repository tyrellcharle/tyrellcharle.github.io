When AI Decides Your Future: The Hidden Problem of Bias

Nov 1, 2026 min read

Introduction

Artificial intelligence (AI) and “Super intelligence” (SI) are becoming a bigger and daily part of our everyday life. Companies and business use it to review job applications, banks use it to make decisions, and hospitals use it to help with healthcare. AI can save time and decision making as well as make certain tasks easier, but that does not mean Ai always gets things right.

One of the biggest concerns with Artificial intelligence and (Ai) is bias. AI systems learn from data, so if the information they are trained on contains unfair patterns or built in bias , this then can become a serious problem especially when AI is used to make decisions that affect people’s jobs, finances, education, and overall opportunities.

In this post, we will take a look at how AI bias happens, why fairness matters, and what organizations can do to prevent these problems.

How Does AI Bias Happen?

AI systems learn by looking for patterns in data. The problem is that not all data is fair or accurate or created equal. If the information used to train an AI system reflects past discrimination or inequalities, malice the system then can learn and repeat those same patterns.

Imagine a company creates an AI hiring tool then started using years of previous hiring records as its base. If most of the people hired were men, the system might start favouring male applicants, even if other applicants have the same qualifications or better.

This immediately showcases a huge problem that AI is not automatically fair just because it uses technology and mathematics. The information people provide and the decisions made in the past can influence how AI works today.

Why Fairness Matters

Fairness means making sure people are treated properly and are not put at a disadvantage because of who they are. However, treating everyone exactly the same does not always mean the outcome is fair ether.

Example, an AI hiring system might use the same scoring process for every applicant, but if it was trained using biased information, the likelihood of people that could still be treated unfairly by the ai is raised.

This matters because daily we continue to give AI more responsibilities and decisions over our lives and livelihoods. People lose their job and opportunities without even knowing a AI was involved.

I believe companies and organizations should care about more than just saving time and money. They also need to consider whether their systems and programs are actually helping people or creating more problems for the regular person.

Important AI Ethics Concepts

There are several ethics concepts that organizations should consider when developing and using AI.

  1. Fairness and Justice: AI should not unfairly disadvantage certain people or groups.
  2. Transparency: Companies should be open about when they use AI and how it is being used.
  3. Explainability: People should have a way to understand why an AI system made a particular decision.
  4. Accountability: Companies and developers should take responsibility when their AI systems make mistakes or cause harm.
  5. Privacy: Personal information should be protected and handled properly.
  6. Human Oversight: People should still be involved in important decisions instead of relying completely on AI.
  7. Beneficence: AI should be used in ways that benefit people and society.
  8. Non-Maleficence: Organizations should try to prevent their AI systems from causing harm.

I believe all these principles are important because companies need to think about how their AI systems could affect people before continuing deciding to use them.

When Amazon’s AI Hiring Tool Went Wrong

A well-known case of AI bias involved an experimental hiring tool developed by Amazon.

In 2018, Reuters reported that Amazon had created an AI system to help review job applications. The system was trained using historical hiring data, which mostly reflected applications from men.

Because of this, the AI started learning patterns that disadvantaged many female applicants. For example, it reportedly penalized some résumés that included the word “women’s” and downgraded graduates from two women’s colleges.

Amazon tried to address some of these problems, but the company eventually abandoned the project (Dastin, 2018).

I think this is a just prime example of why AI needs to be monitored. The developers did not have to directly program discrimination into the system for it to produce unfair results.

The biggest lesson I take from this example is that AI is only as reliable as the information and processes behind it.

AI Governance and Soft Ethics

AI governance is all about having rules, policies, and procedures in place that guide and dictate how AI is developed and used.

A company using AI to review job applications should have built-in safeguards or separate processes to catch bias, prevent unfair decisions, and fix problems when they arise.

However, following laws and regulations does not always mean a company is doing the right thing. This is where soft ethics comes in.

Soft ethics means looking beyond what the law requires and thinking about how decisions could affect others. For example, even if a company is legally allowed to use an AI hiring tool, it should still consider whether the system is fair to all applicants

I also believe and am a firm believer that companies should not wait until someone complains or a law forces them to make changes. They should take responsibility for their technology and think about the possible consequences before problems happen.

How Can We Reduce AI Bias?

There are a few ways we can help reduce bias in AI systems:

  • Check training data to make sure it does not contain unfair patterns.
  • Test AI systems regularly to find possible problems.
  • Have people review important decisions made with AI.
  • Explain how AI is being used and allow people to question decisions.
  • Make sure someone is responsible for fixing problems when they happen.
  • Continue monitoring systems after they are introduced.

These steps will not prevent every problem, but they will reduce the chances of AI making unfair decisions.

AI is a tool and ultimately be used to help people make quicker decisions, not completely replace human judgment. There still needs to be someone who can question the results and take responsibility when something goes wrong.

Conclusion

AI is useful in many areas, but it is still far from perfect. Bias, unfair decisions, privacy concerns, and a lack of accountability can all cause serious problems if organizations and us do not take them seriously.

The Amazon hiring tool is a just a “small” example of how AI can learn unfair patterns from historical data. Companies need to pay closer and more frequent attention to the information they use and how regularly check how their systems are performing.

Concepts such as fairness, transparency, accountability, privacy, explainability, and human oversight make AI more responsible. Governance provides rules and procedures, while soft ethics encourages companies to think beyond what is legally required.

Personally, I think AI has a lot of potential, and I like what it can do, but I’m not sure how much we should trust it or the people responsible for using it. This technology is still relatively new, and we are still learning about what it can do and the problems it can create. For now, all we can really do is educate ourselves, do our research, and hope that the people developing and using AI make responsible decisions rather than using it to cause harm.
